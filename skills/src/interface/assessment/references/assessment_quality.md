# Assessment Quality Criteria

Use this for evaluating an assessment's quality. Quality is about scoped coverage, evidence-grounding, and useful uncertainty.

## Review Questions

1. Do the in-scope and out-of-scope resource lists define clear evaluation boundaries?
2. Does each question identify the in-scope resource group it evaluates?
3. Are the selected categories relevant to the declared scope, with no material relevant category omitted?
4. Does each question invite a specific, grounded answer rather than a vague or purely binary response?
5. Do Pass, Partial, and Fail answers cite permitted evidence references rather than `None`, while Insufficient evidence answers explicitly identify the missing evidence?
6. Are statuses consistent with the answer and evidence?
7. Are impact and confidence calibrated to the consequence and strength of evidence?
8. Are follow-ups concrete and useful for Partial, Fail, and Insufficient evidence answers?
9. Is the status-grouped summary accurate and does its next follow-up prioritize the highest-value unresolved work?
10. Is the assessment concise and free of lenses, role framing, stop conditions, and completion-goal logic?

## Common Findings

- Scope lists omit resource groups that questions evaluate, or exclude boundaries needed to prevent irrelevant analysis.
- A question does not name the resources it evaluates or asks for an answer too vague to ground in evidence.
- Evidence is copied or summarized rather than cited as a path and line range, URL and anchor, or command and exit code.
- A Pass, Partial, or Fail status uses `None` or has no supporting evidence, or insufficient evidence is hidden behind a definitive status.
- Irrelevant categories add work, or relevant quality properties have no question.
- A follow-up does not say what evidence to obtain or action to take.
